## Executive Summary

Songnado should ship a small, high-impact launch portfolio (4-6 hero packs) and use a monthly drop cadence — a strategy empirically validated by SongPop Party's rotating playlist model — rather than dumping 60 packs at v1. Curation should lean hard into 1990s-2000s catalog, which Luminate data shows is both the fastest-growing streaming category (+8% YoY for the '90s) AND the most-listened-to era across all demographics (64% of US listeners). Critically, the existing Deezer ToS legal exposure (non-commercial/family-scope only, unilateral kill-switch) means broad mainstream chart packs are higher risk than niche/catalog/themed packs — the curation portfolio should bias toward thematic differentiation (Stranger Things-revived '80s, anime OSTs, gospel/CCM, Latin party hits) where Songnado can uniquely own the category and where label monitoring is less aggressive. Recommended portfolio: 50-55 new packs, structured as 4 launch heroes + 8 v1.1 expansion + 12 v1.2 expansion + monthly drops thereafter, weighted toward demographic gap-filling (Gen Z catalog, religious music, anime/gaming, Latin) that broadens the funnel beyond the current Anglo-mainstream skew.

### 5. CURATION METHODOLOGY — Practical guidance for the family team based on documented failure modes

**Confidence:** high  
**Sources:** Wikipedia: Name That Tune 1986 arcade failure (era-audience mismatch); Rolling Stone: anti-gatekeeping principle in music trivia; Deezer ToS for non-commercial use; Luminate streaming data for hit-vs-deep-cut balance

QUALITY CRITERIA CHECKLIST (per playlist):
- Track count minimums: Decade packs = 250+ (depth for replay over months); Theme packs = 150+ (tight curation acceptable); Hero/launch packs = 300+.
- Hits vs deep cuts ratio: Target 70/30 (hits/deep cuts) for launch packs. Rolling Stone evidence shows 'no music-buff gatekeeping' is the success principle — guests must recognize SOMETHING regardless of taste.
- Artist diversity: No more than 5% of any single playlist should be one artist (avoids over-indexing on Beatles/Taylor Swift/etc.).
- Era spread within themed packs: Use multi-decade approach for genre packs (Karaoke, Wedding, Road Trip) but tight era for decade/scene packs (Pop Punk = 2001-2008, NOT 1995-2015).

COMMON PITFALLS:
1. Era-audience mismatch (Name That Tune 1986 arcade flopped because 1940s-70s tracks were aimed at teen players) — match decade pack target demographic to era memory.
2. Curator bias toward personal favorites — use objective metric: 'would 70% of THIS PLAYLIST'S target audience recognize this track within 30s?'
3. Too many deep cuts kills early-game engagement — front-load each pack's track ordering with hits.
4. Repetition across packs annoys users (Bohemian Rhapsody in 4 packs).

DEEZER AVAILABILITY VERIFICATION:
- Write a script (or manual spreadsheet) that queries Deezer Search API for each candidate track BEFORE finalizing playlist.
- Known catalog gaps to check: Garth Brooks, Tool, Joanna Newsom, certain Prince catalog, Beatles availability varies, some Bollywood/Anime rights.
- Validate that returned track is the original artist version, NOT a karaoke/cover version (common false-positive on Deezer Search).

30-SECOND PREVIEW QUALITY VALIDATION:
- Some Deezer previews are intro-only (silent or fade-in), useless for trivia. Pre-listen each preview clip during curation.
- Build a 'preview quality flag' column: GOOD (chorus/hook present) | OK (recognizable verse) | BAD (silence/intro/outro). Cut all BAD tracks.
- Some Deezer preview windows start at 1:00 mark by default but track structure varies — songs that hit the hook at 0:45 will preview to a great segment; songs with 2-minute intros (older prog rock) will preview to filler.

HIT-VS-DEEP-CUT BALANCE (the trivia FUN factor):
- 'Oh I HAVEN'T heard this in 10 years' moments are what makes trivia memorable, but require 60-70% of pack to be clear hits first to anchor recognition.
- Solution: track each candidate by 'recall difficulty' (1-5) and ensure pack has roughly: 40% easy (1-2), 40% medium (3), 20% hard (4-5).
- Game-design implication: Classic mode = full mix; Blitz mode = easier tracks (time pressure); Elimination = medium-to-hard (lasts longer).

EXPLICIT CONTENT HANDLING:
- App Store rating: Frequent explicit content = 17+ rating (limits market). Songnado is family party game = target 12+/PEGI 12.
- Curate clean versions where available. Most major hits have clean radio edits on Deezer.
- Flag any explicit-only tracks (no clean version exists) and either: exclude entirely (preferred) OR build a 'Family Mode' toggle that filters them at runtime.
- Hip-hop/Rap packs hardest hit — accept that some iconic tracks (Eminem, Cardi B) may need exclusion or edit-versions.

CROSS-PLAYLIST DEDUPLICATION:
- Maintain master spreadsheet: Track | Artist | Decade | Genre | Pack memberships.
- Allow 'mega hits' (Bohemian Rhapsody, Don't Stop Believin') in MAX 3 packs (e.g., 70s + Karaoke + Road Trip).
- Other tracks: max 2 pack memberships.
- Avoid case where buying multiple packs feels like buying the same content twice.

### 6. ROLLOUT STRATEGY — Hero launch + monthly drops beats 60-pack launch, both for legal and engagement reasons

**Confidence:** high  
**Sources:** SongPop Party rotating monthly playlists pattern (validated industry leader); Deezer ToS unilateral termination clause (legal exposure scales with content volume); MIDiA niche-engagement thesis; User's own memory file on Deezer legal posture

RECOMMENDATION: SHIP 4 HERO PACKS AT LAUNCH, NOT 60. Then monthly drops.

WHY (legal angle): Deezer ToS Section IV is explicit — non-commercial use, family-scope only, Deezer can terminate access 'at any time for any reason' with 'no notice or liability.' At indie scale (<10K MAU), enforcement risk is low — but SURFACE AREA matters. Launching with 60 packs covering chart-active major-label artists raises monitoring profile. Hero packs + drip rollout lets you (a) test Deezer API stability with smaller exposure, (b) pivot quickly if Deezer changes terms, (c) reduce 'looks like a commercial music platform' optics during App Store review.

WHY (engagement angle): SongPop Party — the documented market leader, 100M+ downloads, owned by Gameloft — uses EXACTLY this pattern. Verified: rotating monthly playlists (80s Classics April, Rock Anthems May, 2020s Hits June, Country/R&B further months). Monthly drops drive recurring engagement: users open the app to see 'what's new', creating retention loops. Shipping all content at launch eliminates this hook permanently.

WHY (curation quality angle): 6-person family team building 60 packs in 3 months = average 10 days per pack = rushed quality. Spreading 50+ packs across 12 months allows 1 week of curation + 1 week of validation per pack = significantly better content.

PROPOSED ROLLOUT:

V1.0 LAUNCH (Q3 2026) — 4 hero packs (alongside existing 11):
- 90s Mega Hits, 80s Mega Hits, Wedding Reception Bangers, Road Trip Sing-Alongs
All free. Goal: prove product, generate reviews, establish core gameplay. Legal exposure minimal — packs already large but no esoteric/edge content.

- 70s Classic Rock, 60s Motown & Soul, Disney Animated Songs, One Hit Wonders, Country Crossover Hits, Hip-Hop Golden Era, 2000s Pop Punk & Emo, Yacht Rock
Marketing moment: 'Songnado v1.1 — 8 new packs!' Press hook.

V1.2 (2 months post-launch) — 12 expansion packs:
- 90s Hip-Hop & R&B, Stranger Things & Synth Revival, K-Pop Essentials, Anime Openings (Pro), Christmas & Holiday (timed for Q4), Reggaeton & Latin Pop, CCM, Gospel, Wedding First Dance, Karaoke Crowd-Pleasers, Stadium Anthems, SNL Musical Guest Iconic.

MONTHLY DROPS (months 5+): 2-3 packs per month rotating across categories. SongPop Party model.

MARKETING POSITIONING: 'New pack every month. We curate, you play.' This is a marketing asset, not a limitation. App Store featured-app pitch becomes 'fresh content every month' (App Store editorial loves recurring updates).

ADDITIONAL LEGAL MITIGATION:
- Build the kill switch (per existing memory note) BEFORE shipping. Remote feature flag per pack to disable instantly.
- Surface 'Audio previews provided by Deezer' attribution prominently.
- Frame packs as 'curated by Songnado community' not 'official genre playlists' to avoid implying endorsement.

METRICS TO TRACK PER PACK (post-launch):

WHAT TO PRO-LOCK vs FREE:

- $2.99 individual: Genre/decade packs with clear nostalgia hooks.
- Pro-only: Niche fandoms (Anime, Video Game, Eurovision, Bollywood) — niche audiences pay premium per MIDiA evidence. Pro tier becomes the 'unlock everything' value prop.
